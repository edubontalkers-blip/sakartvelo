Tbilisi Metro Guide for Tourists: How to Navigate the City Underground

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: July 03, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Getting around Tbilisi is easier than many first-time visitors expect, and the metro is one of the best ways to do it. Fast, affordable, and surprisingly straightforward, the Tbilisi metro is a practical tool for any traveler looking to explore the Georgian capital without the hassle of traffic or expensive taxis.

The Tbilisi metro system has two lines. Line 1, known as the Akhmeteli-Varketili line, runs east to west across the city and is the longer of the two. Line 2, the Saburtalo line, branches off from Station Square and heads northwest. Together, they cover most of the key areas tourists want to visit, including the old town, the central business district, and several residential neighborhoods. Station names are displayed in both Georgian and Latin script, which makes navigation much more manageable for international visitors.

To ride the metro, you will need a MetroMoney card. This is a rechargeable smart card available at any metro station from the ticket booths or automated machines. The card itself costs 2 GEL (Georgian Lari) as a one-time fee, and you load credit onto it separately. Each metro ride costs 1 GEL, which at current exchange rates is well under one US dollar or Euro, making it one of the most budget-friendly public transport options in any European or Eurasian capital. The MetroMoney card also works on Tbilisi buses and minibuses, so it is worth getting even if you only plan to use the metro occasionally.

Using the card is simple. Tap it on the yellow reader at the turnstile and walk through. If your balance runs out, you can top it up at the ticket windows inside any station or at self-service kiosks. Staff at the booths are generally helpful, though English is not always spoken fluently, so it helps to have the amount you want to top up written down or shown on your phone.

A few practical things to know before you ride: metro trains run from around 6:00 AM to midnight daily, with trains arriving every few minutes during peak hours. The stations are clean and well-maintained, though some of the older Soviet-era stations are very deep underground, so be prepared for long escalator rides. Rustaveli and Liberty Square are two of the most central stops and good reference points for getting your bearings in the city.

For tourists staying near the old town, the Avlabari station on Line 1 is the most useful stop, placing you within walking distance of the Narikala Fortress, Metekhi Church, and the winding streets of Abanotubani. If you are heading to Rustaveli Avenue for museums, galleries, or upscale dining, the Rustaveli station drops you right in the middle of it all.

Using public transport in Tbilisi is genuinely rewarding. Beyond saving money, it gives you a sense of daily life in the city that taxis simply cannot offer. Pick up your MetroMoney card on your first day, learn the two lines, and you will have confident access to much of what makes Tbilisi one of the most compelling cities in the Caucasus region.
