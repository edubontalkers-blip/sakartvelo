Georgia Budget Travel Guide: How to Travel Cheap in the Caucasus

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: July 13, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Georgia is one of Europe and Asia's best-kept secrets for budget travelers. Nestled in the Caucasus between the Black Sea and the Greater Caucasus mountain range, this small country packs in ancient monasteries, dramatic landscapes, world-class wine, and legendary hospitality — all at prices that will leave your wallet surprisingly full. Whether you're backpacking Georgia on a tight schedule or slow-traveling for weeks, here's everything you need to know about Georgia budget travel.

Getting There Without Breaking the Bank

Flights into Tbilisi International Airport (TBS) are the most common entry point. Budget carriers like Wizz Air and FlyDubai offer affordable connections from European and Middle Eastern hubs. You can also enter overland from Armenia or Turkey, which is a popular and cheap option for backpackers. The border crossing at Sarpi (Georgia-Turkey) is well-used and straightforward.

Accommodation: Sleep Well for Less

Georgia has a thriving hostel scene, particularly in Tbilisi's Old Town. Expect to pay around 15–25 GEL (approximately $5–9 USD) per night for a dorm bed in a reputable hostel. Fabrika Hostel in Tbilisi is a beloved hub for backpackers and doubles as a cultural space with cafes and markets. Guesthouses outside the capital, especially in regions like Kazbegi, Kakheti, and Svaneti, often include a home-cooked breakfast and dinner for around 50–80 GEL ($18–29 USD) per night — outstanding value.

Eating Cheaply and Well

Georgian cuisine is one of the great joys of cheap Georgia travel. A filling meal of khinkali (dumplings) costs around 1–1.50 GEL per piece, and most locals eat 5–8 as a full meal. Khachapuri (cheese-filled bread) runs about 5–12 GEL depending on the style and location. Local bakeries called tone sell fresh bread for under 1 GEL. Avoid tourist-facing restaurants in central Tbilisi and instead eat where locals eat — look for small canteens called stolovayas or family-run spots in residential neighborhoods.

Getting Around on a Budget

Public transport in Georgia is remarkably cheap. Tbilisi's metro costs just 1 GEL per ride, and marshrutkas (minibuses) connect cities across the country for very little. A marshrutka from Tbilisi to Mtskheta costs about 1 GEL, while the ride to Kazbegi runs around 10–15 GEL. For longer distances, shared taxis are only slightly more expensive but much faster. Renting a car makes sense for groups of three or four exploring remote areas like Tusheti.

Free and Low-Cost Attractions

Many of Georgia's most iconic sights cost nothing at all. The Narikala Fortress in Tbilisi, the Jvari Monastery near Mtskheta, and the Gergeti Trinity Church near Kazbegi are all free to enter. Hiking trails through the Caucasus mountains are world-class and require no fees. Wine tasting in Kakheti can be done cheaply at family-run wineries along the main road between Telavi and Sighnaghi.

Daily Budget Estimates

A realistic daily budget for backpacking Georgia is around 60–80 GEL ($22–29 USD), covering a hostel dorm, two to three local meals, and transport. Mid-range travelers spending on guesthouses and occasional restaurant dinners can get by on 120–160 GEL ($44–58 USD) per day.

Georgia rewards curious, adventurous travelers like few other destinations. It's a country where your money stretches far and the experiences feel genuinely priceless.
