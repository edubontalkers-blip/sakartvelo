Gudauri Ski Resort: Complete Guide for 2026 — Everything You Need to Know

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: July 04, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Nestled high in the Greater Caucasus Mountains, Gudauri is Georgia's premier ski destination and one of the most exciting winter resorts in the entire region. Sitting at elevations between 1,990 and 3,276 metres above sea level, this Caucasus ski resort offers reliable snow conditions, dramatic alpine scenery, and surprisingly affordable prices compared to European alternatives. Whether you are a seasoned skier or hitting the slopes for the first time, Gudauri deserves a top spot on your winter travel list.

GETTING THERE

Gudauri is located approximately 120 kilometres north of Tbilisi along the Georgian Military Highway, one of the most scenic mountain roads in the Caucasus. The drive typically takes around two hours by car or marshrutka (minibus). Regular marshrutkas depart from Tbilisi's Didube bus station, with fares costing roughly 10–15 GEL (Georgian Lari) one way. Private transfers are widely available from Tbilisi airport for around 80–120 USD, making the journey comfortable even with heavy ski gear.

SKI SEASON AND CONDITIONS

The Gudauri ski season generally runs from late November through to early April, with peak conditions usually found in January and February. The resort is well known for its generous powder snow, often accumulating impressive base depths of 2–4 metres by mid-winter. The high altitude and north-facing orientation of many slopes help preserve snow quality throughout the season. Skiers regularly praise Gudauri for its light, dry powder snow — a characteristic that draws experienced off-piste enthusiasts from across Europe and beyond.

SLOPES AND LIFTS

Gudauri ski infrastructure has expanded significantly over the past decade. The resort currently operates around 14 lifts, including modern gondolas and chairlifts, servicing over 70 kilometres of marked runs across beginner, intermediate, and advanced terrain. The Sadzele and Kudebi sectors are popular among intermediate skiers, while the Bidara area offers excellent off-piste opportunities for advanced riders. A new interconnected lift system has improved access across different zones, reducing waiting times considerably.

SKI PASSES AND PRICING

One of Gudauri's biggest advantages for tourists is its cost. For the 2025–2026 season, a standard full-day Georgia skiing lift pass was priced at approximately 55–65 GEL (around 20–24 USD), making it a fraction of comparable Alpine resort costs. Multi-day passes offer better value, and beginners can access dedicated learning slopes at reduced rates. Ski and snowboard equipment rental is available directly at the resort for roughly 40–60 GEL per day.

ACCOMMODATION AND FOOD

Gudauri has a growing range of accommodation options, from budget guesthouses to comfortable four-star hotels. Most lodging is ski-in, ski-out or within easy walking distance of the lifts. The resort village has numerous restaurants and cafés offering Georgian cuisine alongside international dishes. Try a warming bowl of khinkali dumplings or khachapuri cheese bread after a long day on the slopes — both are local staples and widely available for just a few dollars.

PRACTICAL TIPS FOR VISITORS

Always check weather forecasts before heading out, as conditions can change rapidly in the Caucasus. Hiring a local guide is highly recommended for off-piste Gudauri ski adventures. Travel insurance covering skiing activities is essential. The nearest ATM is in the village, so carry sufficient cash. Mobile coverage is generally good, though it may be limited on higher runs.

Gudauri combines world-class powder snow, stunning mountain landscapes, and exceptional value for money. It is one of the genuinely unmissable Georgia skiing experiences available today.
