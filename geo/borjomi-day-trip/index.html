Borjomi Day Trip from Tbilisi: The Complete Tourist Guide

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: July 05, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Nestled in the Mtkvari River valley about 150 kilometers southwest of the capital, Borjomi is one of Georgia's most rewarding day trips. Famous worldwide for its naturally carbonated Borjomi mineral water, this small spa town combines healing springs, forested gorges, and fresh mountain air into a single unforgettable excursion.

Getting There from Tbilisi

The most popular and affordable way to reach Borjomi is by marshrutka, the shared minivan system that connects Georgian towns. Minibuses depart from Tbilisi's Didube bus terminal throughout the morning, with journey times averaging around three hours. Tickets typically cost between 10 and 15 Georgian lari each way. Alternatively, a direct train runs from Tbilisi to Borjomi a few times daily, offering a scenic and comfortable ride through the Kartli lowlands. Check the Georgian Railway website for current schedules before you travel. Private taxis or rented cars are a faster option if you prefer flexibility, and the drive along the E60 highway is straightforward.

The Mineral Water Springs

No visit to Borjomi is complete without tasting the famous Borjomi mineral water directly from the source. The central Borjomi Park, located a short walk from the town center, houses the main mineral water fountains where visitors can fill bottles for free. The water has a distinct sulphuric, slightly salty taste that takes some getting used to, but locals swear by its health benefits. The park itself is pleasant and well-maintained, with a cable car offering views over the valley for a small fee of around 5 lari.

Exploring Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park

Borjomi national park, formally known as Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park, is one of the largest protected areas in Europe and a paradise for hikers and nature lovers. The park visitor center is located at the edge of town and provides trail maps, information, and guided tour options. Entry fees are modest, generally around 5 lari for foreign visitors. Trails range from easy riverside walks to multi-day backcountry routes through dense Caucasian forests. Even on a day trip, the shorter loops offer spectacular scenery, including oak and pine forests, mountain streams, and occasional wildlife sightings.

What to Eat and Where

Borjomi has a handful of good restaurants and cafes clustered around the central park area. Try a traditional Georgian lunch featuring khinkali dumplings, grilled meats, and fresh salads. Budget around 20 to 30 lari for a satisfying sit-down meal. Local bakeries sell freshly baked bread and pastries for a quick and cheap snack.

Practical Tips for Your Day Trip

Start early to make the most of your time, ideally catching a marshrutka or train before 9 a.m. Wear comfortable walking shoes since most of the town's highlights require some walking. Bring a reusable bottle to collect mineral water from the park fountains. The weather in Borjomi can be cooler than Tbilisi, especially in spring and autumn, so pack a light jacket. Most signage in the park is available in English, making navigation easy for international visitors.

A day trip Tbilisi to Borjomi offers an ideal blend of nature, history, and local culture. Whether you are sipping from the famous springs, hiking through ancient forests, or simply relaxing by the river, Borjomi delivers an authentic slice of Georgian life well worth the journey.
