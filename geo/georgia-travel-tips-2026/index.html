Georgia Travel Tips 2026 — What You Must Know Before You Go

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: June 29, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Georgia, the jewel of the Caucasus, continues to enchant travelers with its ancient monasteries, dramatic mountain scenery, world-class wine, and some of the most generous hospitality you will ever encounter. If you are planning a visit in 2026, here is everything you need to know before you land in Tbilisi.

Visa-Free Access for Most Travelers

One of Georgia's biggest draws is its remarkably open border policy. Citizens of over 90 countries — including the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union member states, and many others — enjoy visa free entry for stays of up to 365 days. This makes Georgia one of the most accessible destinations on the planet. Always verify your specific country's status on the official website of the Georgian Ministry of Foreign Affairs before travel, as policies can update.

Travel Insurance in 2026

While Georgia does not currently mandate travel insurance for visa-free visitors, purchasing solid coverage before your trip is strongly recommended tourist advice that experienced travelers never skip. Medical care in Tbilisi has improved significantly, but costs for serious treatment or emergency evacuation can be substantial. Look for a policy that covers at least $50,000 in medical expenses and includes adventure activities if you plan to trek in Svaneti or ski at Gudauri. Many reputable international providers offer insurance 2026 plans starting from as little as $2–$5 per day depending on your age and coverage level.

Getting Around Georgia

Tbilisi's international airport is well connected to European and Middle Eastern hubs. From the airport, a taxi to the city center costs roughly 25–35 GEL (Georgian Lari), equivalent to approximately $9–$13 USD at current exchange rates. Always agree on a price before getting in or use the Bolt or Yandex Go ride-hailing apps for transparent pricing. For intercity travel, marshrutkas (shared minibuses) are cheap and frequent, while private taxis or rented cars give you more flexibility for reaching remote regions like Kazbegi or Kakheti.

Money and Budget Tips

Georgia is an excellent value destination. Budget travelers can live well on $40–$60 per day, covering guesthouse accommodation, meals at local restaurants, and transport. The Georgian Lari (GEL) is the local currency. ATMs are widely available in Tbilisi and major towns. Using a no-foreign-transaction-fee card for withdrawals saves money. Avoid exchanging currency at the airport, where rates are less favorable — the exchange booths along Rustaveli Avenue and in the Liberty Square area typically offer much better rates.

Best Time to Visit

Spring (April to June) and autumn (September to November) offer ideal weather and fewer crowds. Summers are hot and busy, particularly in Tbilisi and coastal Batumi on the Black Sea. Winter is perfect for skiers heading to Gudauri resort, which sits above 2,000 meters elevation.

Cultural Etiquette and Safety

Georgia is considered a very safe country for tourists, with low rates of violent crime. Georgians are famously hospitable — if invited to a supra (traditional feast), accept graciously. Dress modestly when visiting Orthodox churches, covering shoulders and knees. Learning a few words of Georgian, such as "madloba" (thank you), goes a long way.

With its unique blend of ancient culture, stunning landscapes, and unbeatable value, Georgia deserves a top spot on every traveler's 2026 bucket list. Plan carefully, secure your insurance, and get ready for an unforgettable Caucasian adventure.
