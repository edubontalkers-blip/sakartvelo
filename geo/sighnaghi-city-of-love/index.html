Sighnaghi: Discovering Georgia's Enchanting City of Love in Kakheti

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: July 17, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Perched on a dramatic hilltop overlooking the Alazani Valley and the distant peaks of the Greater Caucasus, Sighnaghi is one of Georgia's most beloved destinations. Known romantically as the City of Love, this small yet captivating town in the Kakheti region draws visitors with its cobblestone streets, colorful balconied houses, ancient fortress walls, and warm wine culture. Whether you are a history enthusiast, a romantic traveler, or a food and wine lover, Sighnaghi offers an experience that is genuinely hard to forget.

Sighnaghi sits approximately 110 kilometers east of Tbilisi, making it an easy and popular day trip or weekend getaway. The drive through the Gombori Pass is scenic, though the road can be winding. Marshrutkas (shared minibuses) depart from Tbilisi's Samgori metro station for around 10 GEL per person, while a private taxi will cost between 80 and 120 GEL one way. The journey takes roughly two to two and a half hours depending on your route.

The town earned its romantic nickname partly because of its 24-hour marriage registry, one of the first in Georgia, which has attracted couples looking for a quick and memorable wedding destination. The registry office remains a genuine local institution, and it is not uncommon to spot newlyweds celebrating in the town square.

The fortress walls of Sighnaghi are among the best-preserved examples of 18th-century Georgian defensive architecture. Built during the reign of King Erekle II, the walls stretch for nearly four kilometers and include 23 towers. Visitors can walk sections of the wall and enjoy sweeping panoramic views over the valley and vineyards below. Entry to the walls is free, and the views at sunset are particularly spectacular.

No visit to Sighnaghi is complete without exploring the nearby Bodbe Convent, located just two kilometers from the town center. This active Orthodox monastery is the burial site of Saint Nino, the fourth-century missionary who brought Christianity to Georgia. The convent complex includes beautifully maintained gardens, a historic cathedral dating back to the ninth century, and a holy spring chapel situated at the bottom of a forested path. Bodbe Convent is an active religious site, so visitors should dress modestly with shoulders and knees covered. Entry is free, and the site welcomes visitors throughout the day.

Kakheti is Georgia's most celebrated wine region, and Sighnaghi sits at its heart. Local guesthouses and wine cellars offer tastings of traditional amber wines made using the ancient qvevri method, where grape juice ferments in large clay vessels buried underground. Bottles of local wine can be purchased directly from family wineries for as little as 10 to 20 GEL, offering excellent value and authenticity.

The central square of Sighnaghi is lined with restaurants and cafes serving classic Georgian dishes such as khinkali dumplings, khachapuri cheese bread, and slow-cooked lamb. Guesthouses in the area typically charge between 60 and 150 GEL per night for a double room, often including a homemade breakfast.

Sighnaghi is most beautiful in spring when the surrounding landscape turns green, and in autumn during the grape harvest season known as Rtveli, usually in September and October. During harvest, many wineries open their doors to visitors and the entire region takes on a festive atmosphere.

With its romantic atmosphere, rich history, spiritual landmarks like Bodbe Convent, and deep wine culture, Sighnaghi stands as one of Kakheti's most rewarding destinations and a must-visit stop on any Georgian journey.
