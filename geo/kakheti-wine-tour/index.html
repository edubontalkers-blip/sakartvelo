Kakheti Wine Tour: Best Georgian Wineries to Visit in 2024

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: July 08, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Georgia is widely considered one of the oldest winemaking countries in the world, with a tradition stretching back over 8,000 years. At the heart of this heritage lies Kakheti, a stunning eastern region nestled between the Greater Caucasus mountains and the Alazani River valley. If you are planning a Kakheti wine tour, you are in for an unforgettable journey through ancient cellars, amber-colored qvevri wines, and warm Georgian hospitality.

Kakheti produces roughly 70 percent of all Georgian wine, making it the undisputed center of the country's wine culture. The region is home to dozens of wineries ranging from large commercial producers to small family estates, and visiting them is one of the most rewarding things to do in Georgia.

One of the most respected names in the region is Château Mukhrani, though for Kakheti specifically, Telavi Wine Cellar (Marani) stands out as a popular and accessible choice near the regional capital Telavi. Their tours include a walk through extensive underground cellars and a tasting of several signature wines. Entry and tasting packages typically start at around 15 to 25 GEL per person, making wine tasting in Georgia remarkably affordable.

For a more boutique experience, head to the village of Sighnaghi, often called the City of Love. Nearby, you will find Pheasant's Tears Winery, founded in 2007 by American painter John Wurdeman and Georgian winemaker Gela Patalishvili. This Georgian winery specializes in natural, qvevri-made wines using ancient techniques. They offer tastings paired with traditional Georgian food, and their amber wines made from Rkatsiteli grapes are particularly celebrated among wine enthusiasts worldwide.

Another must-visit stop on any Kakheti wine tour is Khareba Winery, located near the town of Kvareli. Khareba is famous for its extraordinary 7.7-kilometer tunnel system carved into the Caucasus mountain rock, which serves as a natural cellar maintaining a constant temperature of around 14 degrees Celsius. Guided tours run throughout the day and include tastings, with entry fees typically ranging from 20 to 35 GEL depending on the tasting package you choose.

For travelers interested in the most traditional side of Georgian winemaking, visiting a family-run cellar in villages like Ikalto or Velistsikhe offers a genuinely authentic experience. Many local families welcome visitors, share homemade wine poured directly from a qvevri buried in their marani (wine cellar), and serve a spread of homemade cheese, bread, and churchkhela. These visits are often arranged informally or through local guesthouses.

Practical tips for your visit: Kakheti is approximately 80 to 100 kilometers east of Tbilisi, reachable by marshrutka (minibus) from the Ortachala bus station for around 8 to 10 GEL. If you prefer comfort and flexibility, hiring a driver for the day costs roughly 80 to 120 GEL and allows you to visit multiple wineries without worrying about transportation. The best time for a Kakheti wine tour is during the Rtveli harvest festival in late September and October, when you can witness the grape-picking process firsthand and participate in communal wine pressing.

Whether you are a seasoned sommelier or simply a curious traveler, wine tasting in Georgia offers a deeply cultural and sensory experience unlike anywhere else in the world. Kakheti does not just offer wine — it offers a living connection to one of humanity's oldest and most cherished traditions.
