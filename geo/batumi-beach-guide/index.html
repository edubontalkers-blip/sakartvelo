Batumi Beach Guide — Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: June 28, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Batumi is one of the most exciting coastal destinations in the Caucasus region, drawing visitors with its unique blend of Soviet-era architecture, modern skyline, and a vibrant waterfront along the eastern Black Sea. As the capital of the autonomous republic of Adjara, this city offers a beach experience unlike anywhere else in Georgia — and once you arrive, it is easy to understand why tourists keep coming back.

The Batumi Beach stretches for several kilometers along the city's coastline, running roughly parallel to the famous Batumi Boulevard. This wide promenade is lined with palm trees, cafes, restaurants, and amusement attractions, making it an enjoyable walk at any time of day or night. The beach itself is free to access for everyone, which is one of its most welcoming features for budget travelers.

One thing to set your expectations correctly: Batumi beach is a pebble beach, not a sandy one. The Black Sea coast in this part of Georgia consists of smooth, dark stones rather than fine sand. Many first-time visitors are surprised by this, so bringing water shoes or sandals is strongly recommended for comfortable swimming. The pebbles can become quite hot under the summer sun, so footwear is essential even for walking along the shoreline.

The best time to visit Batumi for beach activities is between June and September. July and August are the peak months, when sea temperatures typically reach 24 to 26 degrees Celsius — warm enough for extended swimming sessions. Batumi sits in subtropical Georgia, which means the region receives significant rainfall even in summer, so packing a light rain jacket is always a smart idea. The subtropical climate also means lush green hills surround the city, giving the area a distinctly tropical feel compared to the rest of Georgia.

Along the beach, you will find numerous sunbed rental areas operated by private beach clubs. Renting a sunbed and umbrella typically costs between 10 and 25 Georgian Lari per day, depending on the operator and the season. These sections are generally cleaner and better maintained than the fully public areas. The public sections of the beach are perfectly usable, though they can get crowded during peak summer weekends.

For families, the northern end of the Batumi beach strip near the Batumi Boulevard entrance is well-suited, with calmer crowds and proximity to restroom facilities. More lively beach bars and music tend to concentrate toward the central and southern sections of the coastline.

Beyond the main city beach, Adjara offers several quieter alternatives worth exploring. Gonio Beach, located about 15 kilometers south of Batumi near the Turkish border, features a more relaxed atmosphere and is easily reachable by marshrutka minibus from the Batumi central station for approximately 1 to 2 Lari. Kvariati and Sarpi are other small coastal villages in Adjara that attract visitors looking for a calmer Black Sea experience.

Water sports are available on the main Batumi beach, including jet skiing, banana boat rides, and paddleboarding, with prices typically ranging from 20 to 50 Lari per session.

Batumi's combination of accessible beaches, a lively promenade, subtropical scenery, and affordable costs makes it one of the most rewarding seaside destinations in the entire Caucasus. Whether you are a first-time visitor or returning to explore more of the Adjara coastline, the city delivers a genuinely memorable Black Sea holiday.
