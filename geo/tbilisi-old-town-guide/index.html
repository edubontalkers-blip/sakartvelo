Tbilisi Old Town: Your Complete Visitor Guide to Georgia's Historic Heart

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: June 24, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Few cities in the world pack as much character into a single neighborhood as Tbilisi does in its Old Town. Known locally as Dzveli Tbilisi, this ancient quarter sits along the Mtkvari River and has been drawing travelers for centuries with its carved wooden balconies, winding cobblestone lanes, and a unique blend of Persian, Ottoman, and European influences. Whether you have a single afternoon or several days, Tbilisi Old Town rewards slow, curious exploration.

Getting Oriented

Tbilisi Old Town stretches roughly from Liberty Square southward to the Metekhi Bridge. The easiest way to arrive is by metro — take the Rustavelis metro line to the Liberty Square station and walk south along Kote Abkhazi Street. The area is largely pedestrian-friendly, though the hillside streets can be steep. Comfortable shoes are strongly recommended.

Abanotubani: The Sulfur Bath District

No visit to Tbilisi Old Town is complete without experiencing Abanotubani, the famous sulfur bath district tucked beneath the rocky cliffs on the eastern edge of the Old Town. Legend holds that King Vakhtang Gorgasali founded Tbilisi in the 5th century after discovering these natural hot springs — and the baths have been in continuous use ever since.

The district is instantly recognizable by its domed brick bathhouses rising from the ground like small igloos. Natural sulfuric water flows at around 37–42°C (98–108°F) and is believed locally to have therapeutic properties. Visitors can rent a private room for approximately 10–20 GEL (Georgian Lari) per hour at popular establishments such as Chreli-Abano or Gulo's Thermal Spa, with additional charges for a traditional kisi scrub massage, which typically costs 20–30 GEL extra. Public bathing pools are available at some bathhouses for as little as 2–5 GEL.

Narikala Fortress

Rising dramatically above Abanotubani on the clifftop is Narikala fortress, one of the most iconic landmarks in all of Georgia. This ancient citadel dates back to the 4th century and was expanded by Arab, Mongol, and Persian rulers over the following centuries. Much of it remains in atmospheric ruin following an explosion in 1827, but the surviving walls offer some of the best panoramic views over Tbilisi's rooftops and the river valley below.

You can reach Narikala fortress on foot via a steep path from Abanotubani, or take the cable car from Rike Park on the opposite riverbank. The cable car costs 2.50 GEL each way and operates daily. Entry to the fortress itself is free.

The Streets and Squares of Old Town

Beyond the baths and the fortress, Tbilisi Old Town is a neighborhood meant for wandering. Shardeni Street is lined with wine bars and outdoor cafés and is especially lively in the evening. Nearby Leselidze Street (now officially Kote Abkhazi Street) offers local bakeries selling fresh churchkhela and shoti bread. The Anchiskhati Basilica, dating to the 6th century, is one of the oldest surviving churches in Georgia and sits just a short walk from the main tourist path.

Practical Tips for Visitors

The best time to explore Tbilisi Old Town is in spring (April–June) or autumn (September–October), when temperatures are mild. Summer can be intensely hot, so plan outdoor sightseeing for the morning hours. The Georgian Lari is the local currency, and most bathhouses and small vendors operate on cash. ATMs are widely available near Liberty Square.

Tbilisi Old Town is not just a collection of sights — it is a living, breathing neighborhood where history and everyday Georgian life coexist beautifully. Give yourself time to get a little lost here, and you will be rewarded generously.
