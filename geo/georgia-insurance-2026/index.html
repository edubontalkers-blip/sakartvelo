Georgia Travel Insurance 2026: New Law Explained for Tourists

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: July 02, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Georgia, the stunning country nestled in the South Caucasus, has long been a favorite destination for travelers seeking ancient monasteries, dramatic mountain landscapes, and world-class wine. If you're planning a visit in 2026, there's an important update you should know about before you pack your bags.

As of the time of writing, there is no confirmed Georgian government legislation formally titled the 'Georgia insurance law 2026' that mandates travel insurance for foreign tourists as an entry requirement. Georgia currently remains one of the more open and visa-free destinations in the region, welcoming citizens of over 90 countries without a visa for stays up to one year. However, the topic of mandatory insurance for visitors has been discussed in regional travel and policy circles, and requirements can change. Always verify current entry requirements through the official Georgian e-Visa portal (www.evisa.gov.ge) or your nearest Georgian embassy before traveling.

That said, whether or not tourist insurance becomes legally required, purchasing comprehensive travel insurance before visiting Georgia is strongly recommended — and here's why.

**Why Travel Insurance Matters for Georgia**

Georgia's healthcare system, while improving steadily, varies significantly in quality between Tbilisi and rural regions. Private hospitals in the capital such as Aversi Clinic and MediClub Georgia offer good care, but treatment costs can add up quickly without coverage. A basic medical emergency could cost several hundred to a few thousand US dollars out of pocket.

Travel insurance typically costs between $30 and $80 USD for a two-week trip to Georgia, depending on your age, nationality, and the level of coverage you choose. Policies from providers like World Nomads, SafetyWing, or Allianz can cover emergency medical treatment, evacuation, trip cancellation, and lost luggage.

**What to Look for in a Policy**

When selecting mandatory insurance or voluntary coverage for your Georgia trip, make sure your policy includes: emergency medical coverage of at least $50,000 USD, medical evacuation coverage (especially if you plan to hike in Svaneti or Kazbegi), adventure sports coverage if you intend to ski at Gudauri or go white-water rafting, and 24/7 emergency assistance in English.

**Practical Tips for Insured Travelers**

Always carry a printed or digital copy of your insurance policy and emergency contact number. Save the number of the nearest embassy and a local emergency line — Georgia's general emergency number is 112. If you visit remote areas like Tusheti or the Vashlovani desert reserve, ensure your policy explicitly covers off-road or wilderness locations.

Some credit cards offer limited travel insurance benefits, but these rarely provide sufficient medical coverage on their own. Treat them as a supplement, not a substitute.

**Stay Informed Before You Go**

Travel regulations evolve, and it's entirely possible that Georgia could formalize tourist insurance requirements in 2026 as the country continues developing its tourism infrastructure. Georgia welcomed over 5 million international visitors in recent years and has been actively modernizing its travel-related policies.

Check the Georgian National Tourism Administration website (www.gnta.ge) for the latest updates, and consult your travel insurer about Georgia-specific requirements when purchasing your policy.

Georgia is an extraordinary destination — don't let an unexpected event overshadow your experience. Secure solid travel insurance, stay informed, and enjoy everything this remarkable country has to offer.
