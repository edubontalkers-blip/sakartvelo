Best Georgian Wines to Try in 2026: A Tourist's Guide to Tasting the Cradle of Wine

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: June 25, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Georgia is widely recognized as one of the oldest winemaking regions on earth, with a tradition stretching back over 8,000 years. If you are planning a trip in 2026, exploring Georgian wine is not just a tasting experience — it is a journey into the country's culture, history, and soul. Here is everything you need to know to drink well during your visit.

The first wine you must try is Saperavi, Georgia's flagship red grape variety. Grown predominantly in the Kakheti region in eastern Georgia, Saperavi produces deeply colored, full-bodied wines with rich notes of dark cherry, plum, and spice. The grape's name literally means "dye" in Georgian, a reference to its unusually dark, juice-filled flesh. Bottles from respected producers such as Teliani Valley, Kindzmarauli Corporation, or Pheasant's Tears typically range from 15 to 50 GEL (roughly 5 to 18 USD) at local wine shops and restaurants, making quality very accessible for tourists.

Equally important is Rkatsiteli, one of the world's oldest white grape varieties, also centered in Kakheti. When made in the traditional amber or orange wine style using qvevri — the ancient clay vessels buried underground — Rkatsiteli develops complex flavors of dried apricot, honey, walnut, and tannic grip that surprises first-time drinkers. This style of winemaking, where grape skins remain in contact with the juice during fermentation inside qvevri, is recognized by UNESCO as an Intangible Cultural Heritage. Natural wine enthusiasts visiting Georgia will find this style endlessly fascinating.

Kakheti is the undisputed heartland of Georgian wine and deserves at least two or three days of your itinerary. The town of Telavi serves as a convenient base, sitting about 150 kilometers east of Tbilisi and reachable by marshrutka (shared minibus) for around 10 GEL. From Telavi, you can visit wineries in villages such as Tsinandali, Napareuli, and Sighnaghi, the latter being a beautifully restored hilltop town with excellent wine bars and guesthouses.

Beyond the reds and ambers, do not overlook Mtsvane, a fresh white variety producing floral and citrus-driven wines, or Kindzmarauli, a naturally semi-sweet red wine from a specific microzone within Kakheti that is beloved locally and pairs wonderfully with Georgian cheese bread known as khachapuri.

For the best tasting experiences in Tbilisi itself, head to the wine bars along Erekle II Street in the old town or visit the G.Vino wine bar, which stocks hundreds of natural and traditional Georgian labels with knowledgeable staff who speak English. Wine tastings typically start from 20 to 40 GEL and often include small bites of local cheese and churchkhela.

A few practical tips: always ask whether a wine is made in qvevri or stainless steel, as the production method dramatically changes the flavor profile. Buy wines directly from small family wineries whenever possible, as prices are lower and the experience is more personal. Most family wine cellars in Kakheti welcome visitors without prior appointment, though calling ahead is appreciated.

Georgian wine is not simply a beverage here — it is a form of hospitality, storytelling, and national pride. Arriving with some knowledge and genuine curiosity will open doors, fill your glass, and make your trip to Georgia genuinely unforgettable.
