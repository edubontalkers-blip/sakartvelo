Best Restaurants in Tbilisi 2026: A Food Lover's Guide to Eating Well in Georgia's Capital

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: July 01, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

Tbilisi is one of the most exciting food cities in the world right now, and for good reason. The Georgian capital blends ancient culinary traditions with a new wave of creative chefs who are putting their own spin on beloved classics. Whether you're hunting for a cozy wine cellar or a lively neighborhood eatery, knowing where to eat in Tbilisi before you arrive will make a genuine difference to your trip.

Georgian food is unlike anything else in the region. Expect rich walnut sauces, herb-loaded salads, slow-braised meats, and the nation's most iconic dish — khinkali, the juicy soup dumplings that every visitor absolutely must try. Paired with Georgia's famous natural wines, a meal here is a full cultural experience.

**Shavi Lomi (The Black Lion) — Chugureti District** One of Tbilisi's most celebrated restaurants, Shavi Lomi sits in the bohemian Chugureti neighborhood and has earned a loyal following for its elevated take on traditional Georgian recipes. The menu changes seasonally and leans heavily on local, market-fresh ingredients. Try the baked eggplant with walnut paste or the slow-cooked lamb. Expect to pay around 50–80 GEL (Georgian Lari) per person, including wine. Reservations are strongly recommended, especially on weekends.

**Barbarestan — Abanotubani Area** Barbarestan is a genuinely unique Tbilisi restaurant built around a 19th-century cookbook authored by Barbare Jorjadze, one of Georgia's pioneering culinary figures. Dishes are recreated from her original recipes, giving diners a rare taste of historical Georgian cuisine. The setting inside a renovated old Tbilisi townhouse adds to the charm. Budget around 60–90 GEL per person.

**Café Littera — Mtatsminda** Housed inside the elegant Writers' House of Georgia, Café Littera is beloved for its peaceful garden terrace and thoughtfully prepared Georgian food with a modern touch. It's a favorite among both locals and visitors, offering dishes like churchkhela-inspired desserts and beautifully plated pkhali (vegetable and walnut rolls). A meal here runs roughly 40–70 GEL per person.

**Samikitno — Multiple Locations** For tourists watching their budget without sacrificing authenticity, Samikitno is a reliable, casual chain serving hearty Georgian staples including khachapuri (cheese-filled bread), lobiani (bean-stuffed flatbread), and of course khinkali. Prices are very affordable, typically 15–30 GEL per person. It's a great option for a quick, filling lunch between sightseeing.

**Practical Tips for Eating in Tbilisi** Most Tbilisi restaurants accept card payments, but smaller family-run spots may prefer cash, so carry some Lari just in case. Dinner service typically runs from 7pm onward, and popular places fill up quickly on Thursday through Saturday nights. Many restaurants offer outdoor seating in warmer months — a lovely way to enjoy the city's atmosphere.

Georgian hospitality is legendary, and nowhere is that more obvious than at the table. Locals take dining seriously, and meals are meant to be long, generous, and shared. As a visitor to Tbilisi, leaning into that culture — ordering a spread of small dishes, pouring natural wine, and lingering over conversation — is the best way to truly experience what Georgian food is all about.

Whether you splurge at Barbarestan or grab dumplings at a corner spot in the Old Town, Tbilisi restaurants will leave you planning your return trip before you've even finished eating.
