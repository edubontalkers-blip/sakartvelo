Georgia in Winter: What to Do and Where to Go in the Caucasus Cold Season

🇬🇪 Georgia Travel Guide  ·  Published: July 16, 2026  ·  sakartvelo.ai

When most travelers think of Georgia, they picture sun-drenched vineyards and golden hillsides. But Georgia in winter is an entirely different kind of magic — and for those willing to brave the cold, it rewards generously with fewer crowds, lower prices, and spectacular snow-capped landscapes.

Winter in Georgia runs roughly from December through February. Temperatures in Tbilisi hover between 0°C and 8°C (32°F–46°F), while mountainous regions experience heavy snowfall and sub-zero conditions. The country remains very much open for business, and winter Tbilisi in particular has a cozy, lived-in atmosphere that summer tourists rarely get to experience.

**Tbilisi in Winter: Culture, Cuisine, and Warmth**

Winter Tbilisi is perfect for slow exploration. The Old Town's cobblestone alleys, sulfuric bathhouses in Abanotubani, and centuries-old churches feel wonderfully atmospheric in the cold. A soak in a private sulfur bath costs around 15–30 GEL (approximately $5–$11 USD) per hour — exactly what you need after a chilly day of sightseeing.

The city's café culture truly comes alive in winter. Dive into a bowl of khinkali (Georgian dumplings) or warm up with a glass of amber wine in one of Tbilisi's many wine bars. December also brings festive decorations and the Georgian Orthodox Christmas celebration on January 7th, featuring the beloved Alilo procession through the city streets — a uniquely local event worth planning your trip around.

Museums are far less crowded in winter. The Georgian National Museum and the Museum of Fine Arts are both excellent options for a cold afternoon, with entry fees under 15 GEL.

**Skiing Georgia: Gudauri and Beyond**

For skiing in Georgia, Gudauri is the undisputed star. Located about 120 kilometers north of Tbilisi, this high-altitude resort sits between 1,990 and 3,279 meters above sea level, offering reliable snow from December through April. A daily ski pass costs roughly 70–90 GEL ($25–$33 USD), making it dramatically more affordable than comparable European resorts.

Gudauri is accessible by marshrutka (shared minibus) from Tbilisi's Didube station for around 10–15 GEL, or by private taxi for approximately 100–150 GEL. The resort has grown significantly in recent years, with modern quad chairlifts, ski schools, equipment rental, and a range of accommodation from budget guesthouses to mid-range hotels.

Bakuriani is another popular ski resort, located in the Borjomi region about 170 kilometers from Tbilisi. It's smaller and lower in altitude than Gudauri, making it more suitable for beginner skiers and families. A train runs directly from Borjomi town to Bakuriani — a charming narrow-gauge journey in itself.

**Practical Tips for Georgia Winter Travel**

Pack layers. Even in Tbilisi, evenings get genuinely cold, and the mountain regions require proper winter gear. If you plan to ski, rental equipment is available at both resorts, so you don't need to travel heavy.

Georgia winter travel is excellent value. Hotel prices in Tbilisi drop noticeably outside the summer peak, and many guesthouses offer negotiable rates in January and February.

Visas are not required for citizens of most Western countries for stays up to one year, making Georgia one of the most accessible destinations in the region.

Winter may not be Georgia's most obvious season, but it might just be its most rewarding one.
